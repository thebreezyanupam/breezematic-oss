A 16:9 essay about a book or a subject, with pictures, chapters and a voice.

What somebody types at one in the morning having just finished it. Also the subject, when you let it write the script.

Steers the whole video, not just the words: the register of the writing and the feel of the frame.

Script

Chapters: a line starting with # opens one, wherever you put it. Past five minutes an essay is divided into chapters anyway. Each one stops the reading for a beat and types its name over the picture, and the same list becomes the chapters on YouTube. Cinematic look only.

Strength is the “Effect strength” slider below.

Parallax: each picture gets a depth map, made on this computer for free, and the cinematic camera moves the near part past the far part. It also lets a few punch captions — at most one every forty seconds, where a picture has a clear subject — sit in the scene, behind the person or the wall in front, with the same words as a subtitle in the bar. Real sources: real people named in the video appear as their real public-domain portraits from Wikimedia Commons, cut out with a paper edge; and when the book is out of copyright, its text comes from Project Gutenberg, so the script quotes only real lines and every quotation appears on screen exactly as printed. Credits are written next to the video.

A line starting with # in your script becomes a chapter, and the line is never read aloud. On "work them out", the video is cut into parts and named for you.

The opening title and every chapter card. Off by default for military history.

From the free sound library, placed on the exact word and levelled under your voice. 0 is the default level; lower it if they feel loud. Credits go in the description.

Handwritten, small, bottom of the frame

Narration is always the same: the handwriting, caption size, along the bottom where a subtitle belongs. Only the punch changes — bigger, still along the bottom, in whichever treatment you pick, for the few seconds a claim lands. A treatment that never changes is a font choice; one that changes on the punch is editing.

Presets

Saved video settings you can pick again for any video. Settings only — never a topic or a script.

A preset is a named set of video settings — look, pictures, reader, voice, captions, sound and music — that you can pick again for any video. It never holds a topic or a script. Videos made with one are grouped under it in the library.

Settings

Six characters minimum. Your preferences and project history sync; nothing you make is shared with anyone else.

Every key is optional and switches one feature on. They are saved only on this computer, in .local/keys.json, and never leave it except to the service they belong to.

Accounts — optional

Without these, everything is kept on this computer and there is nothing to sign in to. Add your own free Supabase project to sign in and sync preferences and history between computers; run supabase/migrations on it first.

Export keys

Export saves every key in one .env file. Keep it somewhere private; after a reinstall or on a new computer, Import puts them all back at once. The same file also works as the project's .env.
